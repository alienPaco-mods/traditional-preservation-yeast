## 1. What This Mod Does

Vanilla yeast is loot-only. Once the stores are picked clean, no more bread. This mod lets you **grow real vanilla Yeast** from flour and water on a simple wall shelf, the way bakers kept wild cultures alive for centuries.

The result is ordinary vanilla **Yeast**, so it works in every vanilla recipe and every mod that uses it.

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)**, the series' shared mod (TP Core for short). Enable it alongside this one; it loads first.

## 3. Walkthrough

1. **Build the shelf** from the Build menu (Furniture). It mounts **against a wall**.
2. **Make Fermenting Culture** in the crafting menu: 5 flour uses + a glass jar with at least 0.3 L water.
3. **Open the shelf**, drop in up to **5 cultures**, press **Craft**.
4. Come back in **5 days**. Collect the yeast and the empty jars.

That's it. One culture gives one full Yeast packet (10 uses).

---

## 4. The Fermenting Shelf

- **Each press of Craft starts its own timer.** Cultures started together finish together. Ones added later finish later.
- **Holds 5 cultures in total**, counting waiting, fermenting and finished-but-uncollected ones.
- **Cancel** stops every running batch and gives the cultures back.
- **The jars show their progress:** pale when fresh, amber from halfway, **dark brown when done**. A finished jar stays visible until both its yeast and empty jar are collected.

## 5. Secrets & Hidden Features

- **Ferments while you're away**, even in unloaded areas. Leave it running on a long trip.
- **Warmth matters.** Best at 18–30 C. An unheated room follows the season, so a winter kitchen can stretch 5 days to two months. A **basement** swings less and runs a month behind (about 5 C from January to March, 13 C from July to September): 3 to 4 weeks in winter and early spring, a week from July to October.
- **Set the shelf by a fire in winter.** Any lit fire within 2 tiles on the same floor (a fireplace, an antique stove, a campfire, TP Core's Hearth) holds it at 28 C, banked embers at 24 C: **5 days in any month**, for as long as the fuel lasts. Rain doesn't matter. Hover a fermenting culture for its real time left.
- **While the grid is on**, a room is 22 C, as vanilla's are: 5 days.
- **Your jar comes back**, always as a plain Empty Jar, even if you used a crafted one.
- **It's real vanilla Yeast**, so it works in bread, baking and other mods' recipes (including the Vinegar mod's fermentation vessel).
- **One packet is 10 uses.** Vanilla bread dough takes 1 use, so one culture is about 10 loaves.

> *Margin note:* indoors is slower than open air in winter. An unheated house holds the cold average, while a sunny afternoon outside still warms the jar for a few hours.

> *Margin note:* a **loft** (the top storey: a room upstairs with no room over it) reads as indoors, plus three tenths of whatever the 10-day average is above 10 C: the sun on the roof (about 24 C in a 21 C July, no warmer than a room from October to April); 22 C while the power grid is on. A shelf up there keeps the Indoors column from October to April and saves up to a day in May, September and October.

> *Margin note:* the basement is the autumn baker's friend. It holds the summer's warmth into October and November, when an upstairs room has already gone cold; in May it is still cool from the winter.

## 5¾. The Baker's Diary

*Tucked inside a flour tin, the corners dusted white.*

### What a jar is worth

| | |
|---|---|
| One culture | 5 uses of flour + 0.3 L of water |
| Makes | 1 vanilla Yeast packet, 10 uses |
| Bakes | about 10 loaves (bread dough takes 1 use) |
| A full shelf | 5 packets, 50 uses, every 5 days in summer |

| Temperature | Working speed | A culture takes |
|---|---|---|
| 18 to 30 C (by a fire, banked embers, a powered room) | full | 5 days |
| 13 C (a basement in August) | about 60% | about 8 days |
| 10 C | 40% | about 12 days |
| 5 C | 20% | about 25 days |
| 35 C | 60% | about 8 days |

> *Margin note:* a culture never dies of cold here, it just waits. A shelf in an unheated winter kitchen will get there in the end. It just takes two months.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Where does the yeast come from?** It's already there. Wild yeasts live on the grain and in the air, and flour and water give them food and a home. Feed them and wait, and they take over the jar.
- **Why does warmth matter?** Yeast grows fastest at about body temperature or a little below. In the cold it slows almost to a stop but goes dormant rather than dying. Too hot and it suffers, which is why 35 C is slower than 25 C.
- **Why a basement?** Below ground, the earth takes the edge off the seasons: a cellar is mild in January and cool in July, and it runs a month behind the weather: coldest in March, warmest in August. That's why cellars were kept for bread, cheese and beer long before refrigerators.
- **Why by the fire?** Bakers kept the starter crock on the warm side of the hearth in winter, as they set dough to rise by the stove.

### Old names, old ways

- **Sourdough is the oldest raised bread there is.** For thousands of years, bakers kept a little wild-yeast dough back from each bake to raise the next.
- **The Yukon "sourdoughs".** Prospectors in the Klondike gold rush carried their starter with them, some keeping it in a pouch against their chest in winter so it wouldn't freeze. Old-timers in Alaska and the Yukon are still called *sourdoughs* today.
- **Starters are passed down.** Some bakeries and families keep a starter going for generations, feeding it every day.

## 6. Tips

- **Keep the shelf full.** 5 cultures every 5 days is 50 yeast uses a cycle, plenty for a household.
- **Stagger your batches** (press Craft at different times) to always have fresh yeast coming.
- **Put a shelf in the basement for autumn and winter.** About 9 days a culture in October and 2 to 3 weeks in winter, steadier than any unheated room.
- **Flour is the real cost.** 5 flour uses per culture. Plan your wheat harvest with yeast in mind.
- **Hoard glass jars.** Each culture borrows one for 5 days.

## 9. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPY.help()` lists them in the game.*

> **Warning:** They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPY.weather()` | The temperature in each place (open air, under an open roof, indoors, a basement, by a fire, by banked embers), how fast a culture works there, and how many days it takes. |
| `TPY.here()` | Which kind of place you're standing in. |
| `TPY.age(hours)` | Gives every shelf this many hours of perfect fermenting weather. `TPY.age(120)` finishes a culture. |
| `TPY.fast(hours)` | Test timer: a culture takes this many hours. `TPY.fast()` goes back to the sandbox time. |
| `TPY.give(name, n)` | Spawns `n` of: `"culture"` (a Fermenting Culture) or `"flour"`. `TPY.give()` lists them. |

**Typical check:** `TPY.give("culture", 5)`, load the shelf and press Craft, then `TPY.age(60)`. The jars should turn amber at halfway, and `TPY.age(60)` again finishes them.
