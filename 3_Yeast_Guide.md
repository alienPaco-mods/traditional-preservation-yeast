## 1. What This Mod Does

Vanilla yeast is loot-only. Once the stores are picked clean, no more bread. This mod lets you **grow a live culture** from flour and water on a simple wall shelf, the way bakers kept wild cultures alive for centuries, **dry it into yeast cakes**, and crumble them into ordinary vanilla **Yeast**, so it works in every vanilla recipe and every mod that uses it. **Hops** keep a culture going from one batch to the next.

## 3. Walkthrough

1. **Build the shelf** from the Build menu (Furniture). It mounts **against a wall**.
2. **Make Fermenting Culture** in the crafting menu: 5 flour uses + a Jar, Ceramic Jar or Glazed Ceramic Jar with at least 0.3 L water.
3. **Open the shelf**, drop in up to **5 cultures**, press **Craft**.
4. Come back in **5 days**. Collect the **Active Culture (Fresh)**. It's alive and won't wait: about 3 days at 20 C before it's **Spent**.
5. **Shape Yeast Cakes** from it, and get your jar back.
6. **Build a Drying Cloth** (Build menu, Furniture: a Sheet and 2 Stones) where the air moves: outdoors, under a roof, by an open window or by a fire. Right-click it and **lay the cakes on**. Five lots a cloth.
7. About **a week** later, **take off the finished lots** and **Crumble Yeast Cakes**: one full Yeast packet (10 uses) a lot. Dried cakes keep; crumble them when you need yeast.

### Keeping the line: hops

1. **Steep Hops**: a Cooking Pot or Saucepan with exactly 1 L of water + 1 **Hops (Dried)**. Heat it a quarter of an hour on a stove or fire: **Hop Tea**.
2. **Feed Culture**: an Active Culture + an empty jar + 0.3 L Hop Tea + 5 flour uses: **2 Fed Culture**.
3. Back on the shelf, **Craft**: a day later, **2 Hopped Culture**. They keep twice as long. Shape one into cakes, feed the other.

## 4. The Fermenting Shelf

- **Each press of Craft starts its own timer.** Cultures started together finish together. Ones added later finish later.
- **Holds 5 cultures in total**, counting waiting, fermenting and finished-but-uncollected ones.
- **Cancel** stops every running batch and gives the cultures back.
- **The jars show their progress:** pale when fresh, amber from halfway, **dark brown when done**, in the jar you used: glass, ceramic or glazed. A fed jar carries a hop sprig. A finished jar stays visible until it is collected.
- **The shelf's own window tells you the rest:** where it stands, the temperature and the working rate there, a bar for each running batch with its time left, and what is waiting or finished.

## 5. Secrets & Hidden Features

- **Ferments while you're away**, even in unloaded areas. Leave it running on a long trip.
- **Warmth matters.** Best at 18–30 C; at 0 C and below a culture stops and waits (*Paused here (frozen)*). Any lit fire within 2 tiles on the same floor (a fireplace, an antique stove, a campfire, TP Core's Stone Hearth) holds a shelf in a shut room at 28 C, banked embers at 24 C, for as long as the fuel lasts. The Almanac (chapter 6) has every place by month; hover a fermenting culture for its real time left.
- **Your own jar comes back**, the same kind you put in. **Pour Out Culture** empties any culture, spent ones too.
- **A finished culture starts going off the moment it's done**, on the shelf too. By a fire it is spent in about a day: collect it.
- **Wet cakes keep longer than the culture** (5 fresh, 8 to Spent in a cellar), and on the cloth they age more slowly as they dry: laid fresh where the air moves they dry long before they're Spent, in any month (the Almanac). Shaped from an old culture, or left in a shut room, they go off on the cloth and come back as nothing. Taken off early they come back wet, as old as they are. **Dried cakes keep.**
- **Too hot kills cakes:** an hour above 35 C on the cloth and they're dead.
- **The Vinegar mod's fermentation vessel** takes the Yeast too: one full packet a run.

> *Margin note:* most of a winter culture's three months are spent waiting. An unheated room in January sits a little below freezing, and the jar is *Paused here (frozen)* until a thaw. It starts again where it stopped, none the worse.

> *Margin note:* the basement is the autumn baker's friend. It holds the summer's warmth into October and November, when an upstairs room has already gone cold; in May it is still cool from the winter.

## 7. The Baker's Diary

*Tucked inside a flour tin, the corners dusted white.*

### What a jar is worth

| | |
|---|---|
| One culture | 5 uses of flour + 0.3 L of water |
| Shaped into cakes | + 3 uses of cornmeal or flour |
| Makes | 1 vanilla Yeast packet, 10 uses |
| Bakes | about 10 loaves (bread dough takes 1 use) |
| Fed | + 0.3 L Hop Tea (a third of a Hops (Dried)) + 5 flour: one more culture |
| A full cloth | 5 packets, 50 uses, about every 7 days in summer |

| Temperature | Working speed | A culture takes |
|---|---|---|
| 18 to 30 C (by a fire, banked embers, a powered room) | full | 5 days |
| 13 C (a basement in August) | about 60% | about 8 days |
| 10 C | 40% | about 12 days |
| 5 C | 20% | about 25 days |
| 35 C | 60% | about 8 days |

### Why it works the way it does

- **Where does the yeast come from?** It's already there. Wild yeasts live on the grain and in the air, and flour and water give them food and a home. Feed them and wait, and they take over the jar.
- **Why does warmth matter?** Yeast grows fastest at about body temperature or a little below. In the cold it slows almost to a stop but goes dormant rather than dying. Too hot and it suffers, which is why 35 C is slower than 25 C.
- **Why a basement?** Below ground, the earth takes the edge off the seasons: a cellar is mild in January and cool in July, and it runs a month behind the weather: coldest in March, warmest in August. That's why cellars were kept for bread, cheese and beer long before refrigerators.
- **Why by the fire?** Bakers kept the starter crock on the warm side of the hearth in winter, as they set dough to rise by the stove.
- **Why dry it?** A live culture lasts days. Dried in the shade where the air moves, mixed into meal and pressed into cakes, it lasted months: the dried yeast cakes sold in country stores before packet yeast. Too much heat kills it, so it was never dried in the sun or over a stove.
- **Why hops?** Hops are bitter and keep other microbes down, so a hopped culture stayed clean and kept longer. Housewives dried the late-summer hops and fed their yeast from them all year.

### Old names, old ways

- **Sourdough is the oldest raised bread there is.** For thousands of years, bakers kept a little wild-yeast dough back from each bake to raise the next.
- **The Yukon "sourdoughs".** Prospectors in the Klondike gold rush carried their starter with them, some keeping it in a pouch against their chest in winter so it wouldn't freeze. Old-timers in Alaska and the Yukon are still called *sourdoughs* today.
- **Starters are passed down.** Some bakeries and families keep a starter going for generations, feeding it every day.

## 8. Tips

- **Stagger your batches** (press Craft at different times) to always have fresh yeast coming.
- **Put a shelf in the basement for autumn and winter.** About 9 days a culture in October, 12 in November, 17 in December and 3 to 4 weeks from January to March: steadier than any unheated room.
- **Flour is the real cost.** 5 flour uses per culture. Plan your wheat harvest with yeast in mind.
- **Hoard jars.** Each culture borrows one until it's shaped into cakes. Ceramic jars count, and pottery is easier to come by than glass.
- **Plan the shaping.** A culture done in August is spent in 2 or 3 days. Have cornmeal and a free lot on the cloth ready.
- **Dry hops in late summer.** A season's Hops (Dried) feeds a culture all year.

## 9. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Shelf won't place | It must go against a wall |
| Can't add a culture | The shelf already has 5 (waiting, fermenting or uncollected) |
| Culture reads (Spent) | It kept too long or too warm: pour it out and start again, or feed it before it goes |
| Cakes won't dry | A shut room's air doesn't move: open a window. Or it's frozen. Cloth Info says which |
| Cakes came off as nothing | They died: above 35 C for an hour |
| Steep Hops greyed out | The pot must hold exactly 1 L of water and nothing else |
| Culture recipe greyed out | The jar needs at least 0.3 L of water, and you need 5 flour uses |
| Shelf or recipes missing | TP Core must be enabled. Then check `~/Zomboid/console.txt`: the tile number 5127 must be unique among your mods |

## 11. The Yearly Plan

One process, culturing, laid out by place as the game's planting seasons: the months it works there, its best and poor months, and the months it is out of season. A culture takes **5 days** at its best (18 to 30 C) and waits in the cold without dying. The days are the Baker's Almanac's (chapter 6), the grid off.

| Process | Season | Best months | Poor months | Out of season | Where, at its best | Takes, at its best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Culturing in the open air** | April to October | May, June, July, August, September | April (16 days), October (13) | November to March: 29 to 96 days | A shelf outdoors | 5 to 9 days |
| **Culturing in a shut room** | May to October | June, July, August, September | May (8 days), October (10) | November to April: 15 to 97 days | A shelf in a room, windows shut | 5 to 6 days |
| **Culturing in an aired room**, or under an open roof | May to October | June, July, August, September | May (9 days), October (12) | November to April: 15 to 106 days | A shelf by an open window, or on a porch | 5 to 6 days |
| **Culturing in a basement** | All year | July, August, September, October | November to June (11 to 25 days): yet from December to March still the quickest place without a fire | None | A shelf below ground | 8 to 9 days |
| **Culturing by a fire or banked embers indoors**, or in a powered room (22 C) | All year | Any | None | None | Within 2 tiles of a lit fire, a banked Stone Hearth | 5 days |
| **Culturing by a fire outside** (a porch or a campfire) | All year | April to November | December to March (8 to 21 days) | None | Within 2 tiles of a lit fire, under a roof | 5 days |
| **Drying yeast cakes outdoors**, under a roof or in an aired room | May to October | June, July, August, September | May (8 to 9 days), October (12 to 15) | November to April: 16 to 113 days | A Drying Cloth where the air moves | 7 to 8 days |
| **Drying yeast cakes in a basement** | All year | July, August, September, October | November to June (11 to 24 days) | None | A Drying Cloth below ground | 8 to 9 days |
| **Drying yeast cakes by a fire indoors** | All year | Any | None | None | A Drying Cloth within 2 tiles of a lit fire | about 8 days (7 by banked embers) |

> *Margin note:* the baker's year in one line: the open air in spring, anywhere in summer, the cellar in autumn, the hearth in winter. Stock up in summer for the winter's bread and the vinegar vessel.

## 12. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPY.help()` lists them in the game.*

> **Warning:** They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPY.weather()` | The temperature in each place (open air, under an open roof, a shut room, an aired room, a basement, by a fire, by banked embers), how fast a culture works there, and how many days it takes. |
| `TPY.cultures()` | Every culture and wet cake on you or near: the jar, the place, and how long it keeps there. |
| `TPY.here()` | Which kind of place you're standing in. |
| `TPY.age(hours)` | Gives every shelf this many hours of perfect fermenting weather. `TPY.age(120)` finishes a culture. |
| `TPY.fast(hours)` | Test timer: a culture takes this many hours. `TPY.fast()` goes back to the sandbox time. |
| `TPY.give(name, n)` | Spawns `n` of: `"culture"`, `"active"`, `"fed"`, `"hopped"`, `"cakes"`, `"dried"`, `"hops"`, `"flour"`, or any full type such as `"Base.Yeast"`. `TPY.give()` lists them. |
| `TPY.kit(kind)` | The things a test needs: `"build"` (the Fermenting Shelf), `"culture"` (a culture in each kind of jar), `"cakes"` (cultures, cornmeal and a Drying Cloth's makings), `"hops"` (hops, a saucepan of water, a jar, flour and a culture) or `"all"`. `TPY.kit()` lists them. |
| `TPC.cloth()`, `TPC.dryCloth(hours)` | TP Core's: the Drying Cloths near you, and drying their wet lots this many hours. |

**Typical check:** `TPY.give("culture", 5)`, load the shelf and press Craft, then `TPY.age(60)`. The jars should turn amber at halfway, and `TPY.age(60)` again finishes them.
